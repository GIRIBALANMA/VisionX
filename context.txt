PR-01 — Player Movement Heatmap Generation
Problem Statement :
Develop a computer vision system that analyzes football footage to recognize player movement patterns and generate individual and team heatmaps.
 Full Problem Description :
Reserved for your team
The system should track players, analyze their spatial occupation of the field, and visualize movement trajectories and frequently occupied regions.

Objective
Build the detection pipeline in two distinct stages. Do not use jersey color or team identity during the initial person-detection stage.
Stage 1 — Detect and Refine All Persons
The first priority is to reliably identify every football player/person on the pitch, regardless of:

* Jersey color
* Team
* Jersey number
* Player position
* Distance from the camera
* Lighting conditions

The detector must be robust to difficult football-scene conditions, especially:

* Overlapping players — partially occluded players must be treated as separate individuals whenever possible.
* Crowded areas — multiple players close together should not be merged into a single detection.
* Shadows — player shadows must not be incorrectly detected as additional people.
* Background spectators/crowd — spectators outside the playing area should not be confused with football players.
* Partial occlusion — retain a player identity even when another player temporarily blocks part of their body.
* Motion and camera movement — detections should remain stable across consecutive frames.

At this stage, the system should focus purely on:
"Where are the people/players?"
Do not classify them by team yet.
Stage 2 — Identify Players Using Team/Jersey Information
Only after reliable person detection and tracking is established, introduce football-specific visual information such as:

* Jersey color
* Jersey pattern
* Team appearance
* Jersey number
* Shorts/socks when useful
* Other team-specific visual features

Use this information as an additional identity/classification signal, not as the primary person detector.
The team/jersey information should also help resolve difficult situations such as:

* Two players overlapping
* Players crossing each other
* Players entering or leaving crowded areas
* Temporary occlusion
* Similar-looking players
* Identity switching between frames

Required Pipeline
Use the following conceptual pipeline:
Frame → Person Detection → Detection Refinement → Multi-Object Tracking → Team/Jersey Classification → Player Identity Association
Keep person detection and player identification as separate stages.
The system should first establish:
Person A is located here and continues moving through these frames.
Then determine:
Person A belongs to Team X / has Jersey Number Y.
The goal is to prevent jersey color, crowd appearance, shadows, or temporary overlaps from causing missed detections, merged detections, false positives, or player identity switches.


Clear. Two-stage separation, no shortcuts. Here's the breakdown mapped to techniques:

**Stage 1 — Person Detection & Tracking (jersey-agnostic)**

| Sub-step | Approach |
|---|---|
| Detection | YOLOv8/v9 (person class only) fine-tuned on football datasets (SoccerNet) — ignore color entirely |
| Refinement | NMS tuned tight for crowded/overlapping boxes; use Soft-NMS instead of hard NMS so close players aren't suppressed into one box |
| Shadow/crowd filtering | Restrict detection to pitch region via field segmentation (mask out stands/crowd using homography or a pitch-line/greenery segmenter) |
| Occlusion handling | Use a detector that predicts visible + amodal boxes if possible, or rely on tracker to bridge gaps |
| Tracking | ByteTrack or BoT-SORT — good specifically because they recover low-confidence boxes (partial occlusion) instead of dropping the ID |
| Stability | Kalman filter motion prediction + camera motion compensation (ORB/optical-flow-based ego-motion correction, since broadcast cams pan/zoom) |

Output of Stage 1: `track_id, bbox, frame_num` — pure "person exists here, moving like this." No team info touches this stage.

**Stage 2 — Team/Jersey Identity (on top of tracks)**

| Sub-step | Approach |
|---|---|
| Jersey/team classification | Crop each tracked box → color histogram clustering (k-means, k=2 teams + ref/keeper) or a small CNN classifier |
| Jersey number (optional) | OCR (PaddleOCR/CRNN) on torso crop, low-res so treat as weak signal |
| ID re-association | When tracker loses/switches ID (occlusion, crossing), use jersey/team embedding similarity to re-link tracks — re-ID network (OSNet-style embeddings) trained on team appearance |
| Switch correction | Compare appearance embedding before/after occlusion gap to confirm same player, not just spatial proximity |

Output of Stage 2: `track_id → team, jersey_number(optional)` merged back onto Stage 1 tracks.

**Final pipeline:**
Frame → YOLO (person) → Soft-NMS + pitch-mask filter → ByteTrack/BoT-SORT (with camera motion comp) → per-track crops → team/jersey classifier + Re-ID → identity-resolved tracks → heatmap aggregation.

This keeps failure modes isolated — if jersey classification fails, it never causes a missed/merged detection, since Stage 1 already locked in "who and where."
